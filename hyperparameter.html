Hyperparameter Transfer for Large Neural Networks

Keywords: Mathematical Research, Linear Algebra, Probability Theory, Deep Learning, Large Language Models

In the Tensor Programs series, Greg Yang introduced the µ-Parametrization which enables the stability of optimal hyperparameters even as model's width changes (Yang, 2022). This property allows a new hyperparameter tuning paradigm that reduces massively its associated costs. Furthermore, this parametrization is claimed to be the unique parametrization that enables hidden layers to update as much as possible, during the training process, without causing activations to blow-up.


Since recent works to demonstrate these properties are very complex and rely on many assumptions, the core ideas behind the µ-parametrization and the link between its different properties are very difficult to cease.

Project

This academic work was part of a group project at IMT Atlantique, aimed in part at developing a rigorous mathematical proof using elementary tools from probability theory and linear algebra.

My individual contribution was to develop a proof that the μ-parametrization arises as the unique solution of a linear optimization problem derived from the analysis of the infinite-width limit of the outputs and weights of multi-layer perceptrons. Extending this result to large transformer-based architectures is left for future work.
